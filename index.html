Hanoi’s office buildings are paying to cool and filter space no one is using.

HVAC already accounts for the largest controllable slice of a Hanoi office building’s energy bill, and it runs on a fixed schedule that has no idea whether a floor is full or empty.

The building already owns the hardware. What’s missing is the intelligence to use it.

Electricity is only getting more expensive and climate targets only getting stricter. Yet climate control in most buildings is essentially blind: it runs the same way whether a floor is empty or full.

We’re not replacing your HVAC. We’re giving it the intelligence to run itself.

Most “smart building” software stops at recommendations. AirAI is built to close the loop all the way to the equipment itself.

  1. 1

    Sensing

    Real-time occupancy, temperature, and CO2 data from every zone, plus current (CT) sensors that confirm equipment is physically responding, not just what the AI intended to happen.

  2. 2

    Decision-making

    Machine learning (LSTM, XGBoost) predicts each zone’s thermal demand ahead of time; Model Predictive Control calculates the optimal valve position, fan speed, and setpoint before demand peaks, not after.

  3. 3

    Actuation

    Commands are executed directly at the equipment, through the building’s BMS where one exists, or through actuators AirAI installs where it doesn’t, instead of being surfaced only as a dashboard suggestion.

Most smart-building tools end after the second step. The third is where AirAI starts to differ, and current sensors then confirm each command really happened, feeding the next prediction.

    The AI makes decisions without executing them until they are validated against real building operation.

  • Local failsafe

    The on-site gateway keeps equipment running safely if the cloud connection drops.

  • Independently verified savings

  • Encrypted end to end

    TLS with mutual authentication between gateway and cloud.

The actuation layer reaches buildings competitors structurally cannot.

Software-only competitors such as BrainBox AI assume a building already has electronic actuators in place, which excludes most of Hanoi’s office stock, where valves and dampers are still mechanical, pneumatic, or manually operated.

Why offices, and why now.

Office buildings already own HVAC hardware and, increasingly, some form of building management system. The segment doesn’t need new capital-heavy infrastructure; it needs the missing decision-making layer.

That makes it the fastest, lowest-friction segment to prove the model before expanding into malls, hospitals, and residential towers.

  1. Offices, today
  2. Shopping malls
  3. Private hospitals
  4. Residential towers

Three pressures are converging

+17%

Rising energy costs

EVN has raised commercial electricity tariffs four times since early 2023. The cost of leaving HVAC inefficiency unaddressed keeps climbing every year.

2050

Tightening climate commitments

Vietnam has pledged net-zero emissions by 2050 and a coal power phase-out by the 2040s. Fixed-schedule buildings fall further out of step with what regulators, tenants, and investors expect.

15 to 25%

A proven model, not yet local

BrainBox AI has shown the overlay approach works elsewhere, cutting costs by 15 to 25% and carbon by 20 to 40%, but it structurally can’t serve buildings without existing electronic actuators, which is most of Hanoi’s market. That gap is the opportunity.
